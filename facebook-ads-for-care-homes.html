Facebook Ads for Care Homes

Show families what daily life and personal support look like before asking them to enquire. Use real residential-care stories and a clear, low-pressure invitation to learn more.

Talk to AlexRead the strategy

Campaign focus

Help a family picture a visit—not a guaranteed placement.

The ad introduces the home. A conversation and assessment establish whether its support is suitable.

This Facebook and Instagram plan is for homes promoting residential accommodation and personal care. It is not a campaign for every older-adult housing option. Nursing provision, assisted living and retirement housing each need accurate descriptions and offers tied to the setting actually advertised.

For campaigns promoting nursing provision rather than residential personal care, use the nursing home and skilled nursing marketing hub. A mixed-service operator may need both approaches, on clearly separated service pages.

Written and reviewed by Alex · Updated October 2026

Choose a Family Question for Each Campaign

Start with questions heard during real enquiries: What are the rooms like? Can relatives visit? How does the home help someone settle in? What happens on a first tour? One focused answer is usually easier to understand than a montage claiming to offer everything.

For respite, explain how a temporary stay is discussed and assessed. For permanent residential care, introduce daily support and the visit process. Do not present the same offer as both a holiday stay and a care placement.

Create Three Distinct Residential-Care Stories

A first-visit walkthrough

Show the entrance, a representative room and shared spaces. Explain who meets visitors and which questions they can bring. Make clear that a pictured room is not a reservation.

Meet a member of the team

Let a staff member describe a real part of their role, such as helping new residents settle in. Use plain language and avoid suggesting every resident receives an identical routine.

Daily choices and activities

Explain meals, shared activities and opportunities for individual preferences. Show authentic options, not a staged promise that everyone takes part in the same way.

Protect Residents While Gathering Creative

Obtain appropriate permission before using identifiable people, voices or personal stories. Participation should not be treated as a condition of care. When a resident cannot appropriately participate, film staff explanations, unoccupied rooms or other material cleared through the home’s consent process.

Review backgrounds for names, care notes, medication details and other private information. A warm video is not worth exposing someone’s personal circumstances.

Offer a Useful Next Step

Test a home information pack, a visit request or a call about residential support. The destination should explain what the family receives and whether a suitability discussion comes first. An illustrative ad could say “Explore Daily Life at [Home] — Ask About a Visit.” It should not claim to know the viewer’s family situation.

Keep the page specific to the advertised home. Requiring a visitor to search a national directory after clicking a local ad creates unnecessary work.

Use Local Reach Without Guessing Sensitive Needs

Plan around the home’s practical catchment and the people who can arrange visits. Let the creative explain the offer instead of trying to infer a viewer’s health, disability or care needs. Check the campaign’s applicable housing and other policy requirements before choosing targeting controls.

Do not assume that a visitor list from a care-related page is suitable for retargeting. Have the proposed audience, data use and consent process reviewed before activation.

Qualify Gently and Follow Through

Ask for the preferred home, contact method and whether the person wants information, a call or a visit. Keep detailed health and care assessments out of the advertising form. Tell people when a member of the team will respond.

Use the first conversation to understand the requested support and explain the assessment process. Offer helpful next steps when the home is not a fit instead of repeatedly chasing an unsuitable enquiry.

Compare Creative by Completed Visits and Fit

Track which story leads to a reachable conversation, an appropriate visit and further assessment. A room walkthrough may receive fewer forms than a broad promotion but create more useful visits. Review cancellations and suitability outcomes before declaring a winner.

Keep respite and permanent enquiries separate. Record marketing costs alongside response capacity and eventual move-ins; views and likes can explain reach but do not show occupancy impact on their own.

A 90-Day Creative and Visit Plan

First month

Gather approved home footage, choose the visit or information offer, and make the enquiry handoff clear.

Second month

Test walkthrough, staff and daily-life stories. Ask the team which enquiries arrive with realistic expectations.

Third month

Refresh the strongest story with new material and improve visit follow-up. Expand only when the home can handle the interest.

Facebook Ads for Care Homes FAQ

Do care-home ads need residents on camera?

No. Staff introductions, approved room tours and explanations of the visit process can be useful without identifying residents.

What should the first offer be?

Choose a home-specific information pack, conversation or visit request that matches the team’s process. Avoid suggesting that submitting a form reserves a placement.

Should respite and permanent-care enquiries be combined?

Keep their offers and reporting distinct when their assessment, availability and economics differ.

Are likes a useful success measure?

They show engagement, not suitable demand. Review reachable enquiries, completed visits, assessments and move-ins to understand practical value.
