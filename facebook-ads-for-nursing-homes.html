Facebook Ads for Nursing Homes

Explain the facility’s nursing services and admissions process before a family needs to navigate a care transition. Build understanding without implying clinical suitability or targeting someone’s diagnosis.

Talk to AlexRead the strategy

Campaign focus

Education supports admissions. It does not replace referral review.

A social campaign can clarify services and contacts; qualified staff still determine the appropriate next step.

This guide is for facilities providing nursing care, including US SNFs where that designation applies. Its focus is service education, family preparedness and trust in the admissions process—not residential lifestyle tours as a substitute for clinical information. Nursing-home terminology and referral rules vary by jurisdiction.

For residential personal-care enquiries and family visits, use the residential care home marketing hub. Assisted living and retirement housing need their own accurate service descriptions, not a nursing label.

Set a Role for Social Alongside Referrals

A person scrolling Facebook is not necessarily ready to choose a nursing facility. Use campaigns to answer practical questions and introduce the admissions route. Do not judge every educational video by immediate admissions, or assume paid social can replace relationships with referral professionals.

Keep family education separate from professional outreach. A family guide might explain what to ask admissions; a referral professional needs accurate capabilities and the approved contact or document route.

Explain Nursing Services Without Blurring Care Levels

Create separate messages for long-term nursing support and short-stay skilled or rehabilitation services when both are actually provided. Explain the purpose of each service and who reviews an enquiry. A general retirement-lifestyle ad does not answer a question about nursing capability.

Use SNF only for the appropriate US context. Canadian long-term care and UK nursing-home language should follow the facility’s setting rather than copying American certification or payment claims.

Build Creative Around the Admissions Questions

What happens after first contact?

An admissions representative explains the next steps, how families can ask questions and when a professional referral may be needed. Avoid naming individual patients.

What does the service include?

A qualified staff member describes a verified nursing or therapy service and its limits. Show only approved equipment and spaces; do not imply that a demonstration predicts recovery.

How can families prepare?

Offer questions to ask about visiting, communication, referral review and payment verification. Frame the material as practical preparation, not a medical assessment.

Avoid Clinical Promises and Personal-Attribute Copy

Do not use claims such as guaranteed recovery, automatic acceptance or a covered stay. Avoid copy that tells the viewer they or a relative have a diagnosis. An illustrative alternative is “Learn About Nursing Services at [Facility] — Speak With Admissions.” Staff should confirm the description before publication.

Use the facility’s clinical and compliance review process for testimonials and service explanations. A single person’s experience is not evidence that another person will have the same outcome.

Separate Marketing Forms From Referral Documents

A social form can request contact details and a preferred way to speak with admissions. It should not collect diagnoses, discharge summaries, insurance documents or medication lists. Direct referral material to the facility’s approved secure process.

Inspect tracking as well as the visible form. Page URLs, event names and free-text fields can expose sensitive context. Do not upload patient or referral lists to create advertising audiences without an appropriate review of applicable rules; consent alone does not override a platform restriction.

Review Targeting Before Launch

The advertised service and jurisdiction affect which platform categories and controls apply. Do not build a plan around guessed health interests or narrowly inferred patient groups. Review the current Meta requirements for the actual service before selecting audiences or adding retargeting.

Keep professional relationship-building outside a consumer ad funnel when that is the better route. Public educational content can help families understand the facility without pretending that social targeting can identify clinical suitability.

Make the Handoff to Admissions Reliable

Define who receives an enquiry, what acknowledgement is sent and how the next conversation is recorded. A social response team should not make clinical or placement decisions. Explain how to contact admissions during staffed hours and what happens when that team is unavailable.

When a referral is received, preserve source information inside the approved internal process. A long review cycle should not be misreported as a failed ad merely because an admission did not happen within a short platform attribution window.

Measure Understanding and Appropriate Progression

Use reach and video engagement to understand whether educational material is being seen. Evaluate contact campaigns by relevant admissions conversations and referral progression. Keep those measures distinct so an awareness campaign is not presented as proven admission revenue.

Compare clinical service fit, referral completion and lost reasons inside secure operational reporting. Share only approved, necessary measurement data with advertising tools. A less detailed external report is preferable to exposing a person’s care information.

A 90-Day Nursing Education Campaign Plan

Weeks 1–4

Choose the nursing pathway, review privacy and platform constraints, and record approved staff explanations.

Weeks 5–8

Publish service and admissions education. Check whether the team receives clearer questions and correctly routed contacts.

Weeks 9–12

Revise confusing explanations, review appropriate referral progression and decide whether paid social adds value alongside search and referral work.

Facebook Ads for Nursing Homes FAQ

Can Facebook ads replace hospital referral relationships?

No. They can support public understanding and appropriate enquiries, but referral coordination and clinical review remain separate operational work.

Should we advertise short-stay and long-term care together?

Only if the message clearly distinguishes them. Separate creative and destination pages usually make service limits and admissions steps easier to explain.

Can a patient outcome be used as an ad promise?

Do not imply that one person’s outcome will apply to others. Have testimonials and clinical claims reviewed before use and obtain the appropriate permissions.

What makes a useful nursing-home social lead?

A relevant, reachable contact routed to the correct admissions team is a useful first step. It is not proof of clinical suitability, funding eligibility or acceptance.
