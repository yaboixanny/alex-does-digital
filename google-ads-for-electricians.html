Written and reviewed by Alex · Updated October 2026

01

Separate campaigns by service intent

Keep urgent electrical repair distinct from planned work such as panel upgrades, EV chargers, rewiring, lighting, generators, and commercial projects. Start with a focused core electrician campaign, then create tightly themed ad groups or campaigns for services such as emergency repair, panel upgrades, EV chargers, generators, rewiring, and light-fixture installation when each has enough budget and a relevant page.

02

Choose keywords and negatives carefully

Use Keyword Planner and real search-term data to build service-specific themes. Exact and phrase match offer more steering, while broad match reaches further and should be paired with trustworthy conversion data and an appropriate bidding strategy. Review searches for jobs, training, DIY, wholesale parts, products, salaries, and unsupported services before applying negatives.

03

Target profitable service areas

Match locations to technician coverage and travel economics. Review the advanced location option instead of accepting the default automatically: a local service campaign may favor people in or regularly in the included area rather than everyone who merely showed interest in it. Exclude places the team cannot serve and schedule ads around real call coverage.

04

Use the closest landing page

Send each service theme to a dedicated landing page rather than a generic homepage or contact page. Name the electrical service and location, explain the process, show licensing, insurance, permit knowledge, safety process, warranties, service coverage, and completed projects, answer price and timing questions, and make calling or requesting an estimate easy on mobile.

05

Budget for a meaningful test

Use local click estimates as an input, then work backward from the number of clicks, qualified leads, close rate, and job value needed for a useful test. Do not treat a fixed daily amount or “ten clicks per day” as a universal rule, and do not divide a small budget across every electrical service.

06

Track completed work

Track calls from ads, calls from the website, forms, booked appointments, and completed jobs separately. Record service needed, safety symptoms, property type, ownership, location, panel details, permits, timeline, revenue, and lost reasons so the account optimizes toward useful work rather than every phone click.

07

Optimize in stages

First verify tracking, search terms, geography, schedules, pages, and phone handling. Then compare services, devices, ads, assets, and bidding. Manual CPC and Maximize Clicks remain available, but the right starting strategy depends on the account; conversion-based bidding needs reliable conversion actions and enough meaningful data.

Campaign build

Build Ad Groups Around Electrical Services

A core ad group might cover electrician, local electrician, residential electrician, and qualified “near me” intent. Additional ad groups should reflect services people actually book, such as emergency electrician, light-fixture installation, panel upgrades, EV chargers, generators, or rewiring. The keyword, responsive search ad, landing page, and conversion action should all describe the same job.

Google notes that phrase match can reach more searches than exact match, while exact match provides more steering. Broad match reaches further and uses additional signals, so it should be tested with strong conversion measurement and bidding rather than added by default. Review the official keyword match guidance and inspect the search terms report regularly.

Use negative keywords deliberately

Common themes to evaluate include careers, apprenticeships, salaries, courses, certification, DIY instructions, diagrams, parts, wholesale supplies, and services the company does not offer. Review the actual query and intent before blocking a word across the account; an overly broad negative can remove useful traffic.

Ads and assets

Make the Service, Location, and Next Step Obvious

Responsive search ads should combine the electrical service, service area, credible proof, availability, relevant pricing context, and a direct action such as calling or booking an estimate. Verify the business name and logo where eligible. Add call assets so phone demand is measurable, and use sitelinks and callouts that remain relevant at the level where they are applied.

Asset scope matters. A panel-upgrade ad group should not inherit a callout or sitelink that only makes sense for a different electrical service. Apply assets at the account, campaign, or ad-group level based on where the message is genuinely accurate, then evaluate their results with the rest of the conversion path.

Controls and measurement

Choose Locations, Bidding, and Conversions Together

Google's default advanced location option can include physical presence and location interest. Local electricians should review whether the “presence” option better matches their service model, while remembering that location targeting is based on imperfect signals. See Google's location-targeting guidance and verify results by actual lead location.

Do not select a bid strategy in isolation. Maximize Clicks is designed to seek clicks within the budget and can use a campaign-level CPC limit; Manual CPC gives direct bid control; conversion-focused strategies depend on trustworthy conversion data. Google's bidding guidance describes those tradeoffs. Whichever strategy you use, track phone calls and forms, then import qualified or completed outcomes when possible. Google's phone-call conversion guidance explains the available call-tracking methods.

Lead quality

Qualify Leads Before Optimizing for Volume

Define a qualified lead using service, location, customer fit, timing, and commercial value. Review call recordings or lead outcomes where legally appropriate, label spam and unsupported requests, and send booked or completed outcomes back into reporting. Cheap submissions can mislead optimization when they do not become work.

Improve the follow-up and conversion path

Make the next step obvious, confirm inquiries immediately, and respond with a knowledgeable human as quickly as the operation can sustain. Track contact attempts, estimates, bookings, completed work, revenue, and lost reasons. Advertising cannot compensate for unanswered calls or unclear scheduling.

Avoid wasted effort

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Do not target every service and location at once, publish unsupported claims, ignore mobile usability, or judge success from surface-level platform totals. Build a focused program, document changes, allow enough useful data, and connect decisions to qualified and completed work.

Frequently asked questions

Google Ads for Electrician Companies FAQ

Do Google Ads strategies work for electrician companies?

They can when the service, market, proof, conversion path, follow-up, and measurement are aligned.

How should results be evaluated?

Use qualified leads, bookings, completed jobs, revenue, acquisition cost, and lost reasons rather than raw clicks or forms.

Should urgent and planned services be separated?

Usually. Their customer intent, message, page, action, value, and follow-up are different.

What should happen first?

Define priority services, coverage, qualification, tracking, and the conversion path before increasing traffic.

What budget should an electrician use for Google Ads?

Use local click estimates, service value, expected conversion rate, close rate, and a defined test window. There is no universal daily minimum that works in every market.

Should electrician ads go to the homepage?

Usually not. Send each tightly themed ad group to the closest service landing page with relevant proof, coverage, pricing context, and a clear call or estimate action.
