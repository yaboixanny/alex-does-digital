Google Ads for Roadside Assistance: Get More Qualified Calls

Build service-specific campaigns for jump-starts, vehicle lockouts, flat-tire help, fuel delivery, winch-outs, and other urgent roadside calls.

Quick answer

Google Ads for roadside assistance works best when each service has focused keywords and ads, targeting matches the real coverage area, phone calls are easy to make, and booked jobs—not clicks—guide optimization.

Roadside assistance is an urgent, local service. A stranded driver may need help now, but the exact need can vary. One person needs a battery jump. Another is locked out or has a flat tire. Your campaign must match that need quickly.

This is why roadside assistance deserves its own page and campaign plan. It overlaps with towing, but the search intent is different. A towing customer needs the vehicle moved. A roadside customer often wants the problem solved where the vehicle is sitting.

Why Roadside Assistance Needs a Separate Google Ads Campaign

A separate campaign gives you better control over budget, service areas, hours, keywords, and call reporting. It also prevents broad towing searches from using the money set aside for high-value roadside jobs.

Keep towing and roadside assistance connected at the business level, but separate at the campaign and landing-page level. If you also transport vehicles, use the broader Google Ads for towing companies strategy for tow-truck searches.

Battery jump-starts

Target drivers whose vehicle will not start and who need mobile battery help.

Vehicle lockouts

Use clear ads for car lockout help without implying locksmith services you do not offer.

Flat-tire assistance

Match searches for spare-tire installation and mobile tire changes within your coverage area.

Emergency fuel delivery

Explain the fuel types, delivery limits, and areas you can serve.

Winch-outs

Describe the situations and vehicles your equipment can safely handle.

On-site diagnostics

Promote this only when your team truly offers mobile assessment or minor roadside fixes.

Structure Roadside Assistance Google Ads by Service

Start with one campaign for the real service area. Inside it, create a focused ad group for each core service. Match the keywords, ad copy, and landing-page section to the same need.

  1. Choose the services: Include only jobs your team can complete reliably.
  2. Group related searches: Keep jump-start, lockout, tire, fuel, and winch-out terms separate.
  3. Write service-specific ads: State the service, location, hours, and next step.
  4. Send traffic to the right page: Do not send every roadside query to a generic towing homepage.
  5. Track qualified calls: Record whether the driver was inside the coverage area and booked service.

Simple rule: A driver searching for a jump-start should see a jump-start ad and reach a page that immediately confirms jump-start service.

Target Only the Locations You Can Reach

Roadside assistance has a hard service-area limit. Target the cities, ZIP codes, or radius that your drivers can cover at a profitable response time. Exclude areas that produce calls your team cannot serve.

Google notes that location targeting uses several signals and is not perfectly accurate. Review geographic performance and actual call locations instead of assuming the setting will prevent every out-of-area lead. See Google's official guidance on geographic targeting.

Make Calling Easy Without Skipping Qualification

Use call assets so a mobile searcher can contact the business from the ad. The landing page should also have a large tap-to-call button, the service area, hours, and a short list of services above the fold.

The dispatcher should quickly ask for the location, vehicle, requested service, safety situation, and payment method. This protects the driver, reduces wasted trips, and helps the campaign learn which calls become paid jobs.

What the landing page should show

  • The roadside services currently available
  • The cities, ZIP codes, or coverage radius
  • Service hours and realistic response information
  • A tap-to-call button and a backup contact option
  • Trust details such as reviews, team photos, and accepted payment types
  • A link to towing services when vehicle transport is needed

Track Qualified Calls and Completed Roadside Jobs

Raw call volume can be misleading. A short wrong-number call and a paid lockout job should not have the same value. Track which keyword and ad generated the call, then connect the call to its outcome.

Google Ads supports calls from ads, calls from a website, clicks on mobile phone numbers, and imported call outcomes. Choose the setup that matches your process and privacy requirements. Google's official phone-call conversion tracking guide explains the available methods.

  • Qualified call: The driver needs a service you provide inside your coverage area.
  • Booked job: The caller agrees to dispatch and pricing.
  • Completed job: The technician completes the roadside service.
  • Revenue: The final job value is tied back to the lead source when possible.

Common Roadside Assistance Campaign Mistakes

  • Mixing towing, lockout, battery, and tire searches in one broad ad group
  • Targeting a larger area than the team can cover
  • Running ads when no one can answer or dispatch
  • Sending every click to a generic homepage
  • Counting all calls as equal without checking call quality
  • Using claims about response time, availability, or price that the business cannot support
  • Failing to review search terms and add careful negative keywords

Google Ads for Roadside Assistance: Frequently Asked Questions

Do Google Ads work for roadside assistance?

Yes. Google Ads can reach nearby drivers who are actively searching for a jump-start, lockout, tire change, fuel delivery, or other roadside service. Results depend on local demand, coverage, response time, and campaign quality.

Which roadside assistance services should have separate ad groups?

Build separate ad groups for services with distinct searches, such as jump-starts, vehicle lockouts, flat-tire help, fuel delivery, and winch-outs. Separate groups make the keyword, ad, and landing-page message more relevant.

Should roadside assistance and towing use the same campaign?

Usually, separate campaigns provide better budget and message control. Towing searches ask for vehicle transport, while roadside searches often ask for on-site help. A business that offers both can connect the pages while keeping campaigns distinct.

How should roadside assistance calls be tracked?

Track calls from ads and the website, then record whether each call was serviceable and became a paid job. Qualified calls and completed services are more useful optimization goals than clicks or raw call volume.
