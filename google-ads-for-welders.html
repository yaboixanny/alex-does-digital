Google Ads for Welders & Welding Companies

Get in front of people searching for the welding work you actually do—from mobile repairs to custom fabrication—without paying for every vague search for “welding.”

Google Ads can help a mobile welder or welding company reach customers who are ready to ask for a quote. But a search for “welding” could mean training, equipment, a job opening, or a specific repair. The goal is not more clicks. It is more suitable jobs in the right service area.

This guide turns Alex’s welding campaign walkthrough into a practical plan. It also includes a separate 90-day client snapshot from another walkthrough, with the limits of those numbers stated clearly.

Start With the Welding Jobs You Want

List the services your team can deliver well, the average value of each job, travel time, and how quickly you can respond. Mobile welding, gate repair, marine welding, custom metal fabrication, pipe work, aluminum welding, and shop-based fabrication may all call for different ads. Only advertise a service if you actually offer it and can take on more work.

Choose a first priority instead of dividing a small budget across every service. A mobile welder who wants on-site repair calls may start there. A fabrication shop may prioritize high-value custom projects even if they generate fewer inquiries.

Build Google Ads Campaigns Around Welding Services

Group searches by the customer’s job, not by a long list of loosely related keywords. This keeps each ad and landing page specific. Separate services further when they need different budgets, coverage areas, or sales follow-up.

If the customer searches for mobile welding, send them to a mobile welding page—not a homepage that makes them hunt for the service. A good page has a clear call button, a short estimate form, service-area details, real project photos, and answers about what work you can and cannot do.

Choose Welding Keywords With Hiring Intent

Research the phrases people use in your actual market, then start with terms tied to the services you sell: “mobile welding services,” “mobile welder near me,” “welding repair near me,” “gate welding repair,” or “custom metal fabrication.” Add location variants where they reflect real demand. Avoid assuming a generic “welding” keyword means someone wants to hire you.

Exact and phrase match can be useful starting controls, but neither is a perfect filter: Google matches on meaning as well as wording. Review the actual search terms and add negatives for irrelevant intent such as classes, certification, jobs, salaries, supplies, machines, DIY, and equipment sales—after checking whether those words ever appear in valuable searches. Expand only when the lead data supports it.

Target the Areas and Hours You Can Serve

A mobile welder’s practical service area may differ from a shop that takes larger fabrication jobs. Set locations around profitable travel, exclude areas you cannot serve, and review where inquiries actually come from. For a local-only campaign, consider the location option focused on people in or regularly in your target areas. Google’s location signals are estimates, so keep checking results.

If calls drive most sales, schedule ads around times your team can answer or arrange dependable after-hours response. If you can take forms at any hour, say when the customer should expect a reply. Do not create more paid calls than the team can handle.

Write Ads That Match the Service

Put the actual service in the ad. A mobile welding ad should explain where you work and what kind of on-site jobs you take. A fabrication ad can point to finished projects and the quote process. Mention a city, same-day availability, years in business, insurance, or specialist capability only when each claim is true.

Use helpful assets such as a call option, business name, logo, and links to project work, service details, or contact information. Test messages about availability, experience, and job type instead of writing several nearly identical ads.

Set a Budget and Bidding Plan You Can Measure

Base the daily budget on the days ads run, not simply on a 30-day calendar month. For example, a $1,000 test over 22 advertising days is about $45 per active day. Local click prices and project margins vary, so a budget that works for one welder is not a benchmark for another.

Start with a bidding approach you can monitor against real conversions. Manual click control can suit a tightly managed test; conversion-focused bidding can become more useful when tracking is reliable and there is enough meaningful data. Alex’s client example below used a conversion-focused approach and later tested a target cost per acquisition. No one bidding setting is automatically best for every welding company.

Track Calls, Forms, and Booked Welding Jobs

Set up tracking before spending. Count calls and submitted forms, then record whether the inquiry was for a service you offer, whether someone answered, whether an estimate was booked, and whether the job closed. A call lasting 60 seconds can be a useful reporting threshold, but it does not prove the caller became a customer.

Measure the full path: search → call or form → qualified estimate → booked job → revenue. Cost per tracked conversion is a starting signal; cost per booked job tells you much more.

A Simple First-Month Launch Plan

  1. Before launch: choose one or two profitable services, define coverage, build matching pages, and test call and form tracking.
  2. Weeks 1–2: check search terms, location reports, phone coverage, and whether inquiries match the intended jobs.
  3. Weeks 3–4: improve ads and pages, refine negatives, and compare cost per qualified estimate—not just cost per click.

Frequently asked questions

Google Ads for Welders FAQ

Do Google Ads work for welding companies?

They can help when you target services you actually offer, keep the geography practical, use relevant landing pages, and follow calls through to booked work. Results vary by market and business.

What are the best Google Ads keywords for a mobile welder?

Start with hiring-intent terms such as “mobile welder near me,” “mobile welding services,” and specific repairs you perform. Review real search terms and remove irrelevant intent.

Should mobile welding and custom fabrication share one ad group?

Usually not. The customer needs, project value, ad message, and landing page differ. Separate them when that helps you control budget and measure lead quality.

How much should a welding company spend on Google Ads?

There is no universal amount. Set a test budget based on local click costs, the number of active advertising days, your job margins, and how many qualified inquiries you need to judge results.

Is a 60-second call a qualified lead?

Not necessarily. Duration can filter out very short calls, but only a call review or CRM record can show whether the inquiry matched your services and became an estimate or job.
