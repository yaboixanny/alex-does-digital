Google Local Services Ads for Towing: Cost Per Lead Benchmarks

A sourced 2026 guide to towing LSA cost per lead, what the benchmark actually measures, and how Local Services Ads compare with Google Search cost per call.

Short answer

Plan around $40–$90 per charged LSA lead.

That is a directional home-services planning range, not an official towing guarantee. The number that matters is cost per qualified, booked, and completed towing job in your market.

A realistic 2026 planning range for Google Local Services Ads for towing is approximately $40 to $90 per charged lead. Treat that range as a starting hypothesis, not a promise. Google does not publish a national towing-specific average, and its own documentation says Local Services lead prices vary by location, job type, lead type, and bidding mode.

Third-party 2026 benchmark summaries place the median Local Services Ads cost per lead across multiple home-service trades near $40 and a broader working range around $45 to $90. Towing can land outside that range because emergency demand, competition, call availability, geography, and the services enabled all change pricing.

Benchmark methodology

The $40–$90 range combines publicly reported home-services LSA benchmarks with Google’s explanation of variable lead pricing. It is not derived from a Google-published towing dataset. Local Services Ads vs. Google Ads Cost Per Call

Local Services Ads and standard Google Search campaigns use different billing and reporting models. LSA generally charges for leads delivered through the Local Services unit. Standard Search Ads charge for clicks, after which the landing page and call experience determine whether a conversion occurs.

The towing case study on this site produced calls at $18.53 with a 35% conversion rate and a $55 daily budget. That is an unusually efficient individual Search campaign result, not a universal market benchmark. It should not be relabeled as an LSA cost per lead because the campaign, billing model, placements, and optimization controls were different.

How Google Sets Local Services Ads Lead Costs

Google says lead price can change with the advertiser’s location, the type of job, the type of lead, and bidding mode. Local Services Ads supports automated “Maximize leads” bidding and a target cost-per-lead option. A weekly budget guides how many leads the system attempts to deliver, while the monthly maximum limits total spend.

That means two towing companies can pay different amounts even when they operate in nearby markets. One may enable towing, lockouts, tire changes, and jump starts across a wide radius, while another accepts only towing inside a smaller service area.

LSA dashboard CPL is not the final economic metric. A charged lead still needs to be answered, qualified, accepted, booked, dispatched, and completed.

What Counts as a Qualified Towing LSA Lead?

Define qualification before judging the platform. A useful lead normally matches the supported service, pickup area, vehicle type, schedule, truck capability, and payment requirements. Track pickup location, destination, requested service, whether the job was accepted, whether it was completed, revenue, and lost reason.

Separate towing from roadside assistance. Lockouts, jump starts, fuel delivery, and tire changes may carry different prices and close rates from a tow. Blending them makes the average CPL difficult to interpret.

How to Lower Towing LSA Cost Per Qualified Lead

Complete the business profile accurately, enable only services the company performs, keep operating hours current, and use a service area the fleet can reach. Answer quickly and train dispatchers to identify lead source and qualification consistently. Collect honest reviews after completed work and keep license, insurance, and screening information current where required.

Do not chase a lower dashboard CPL by accepting every inquiry as valuable. A higher CPL can be profitable when the calls match available trucks and produce completed jobs. The optimization target should be cost per completed job and revenue—not the cheapest lead notification.

When to Use LSA, Search Ads, or Both

Use Local Services Ads when towing is eligible in the market, the profile is competitive, calls can be answered reliably, and lead-level economics are measurable. Use Search Ads when you need more control over services, keywords, negatives, landing pages, geography, schedules, and conversion tracking.

Many towing companies should test both separately. Keep channel reporting distinct, use consistent qualification, and compare qualified calls, bookings, completed jobs, and revenue over the same period.

Frequently asked questions

Towing LSA Cost Per Lead FAQ

What is a realistic towing LSA cost per lead in 2026?

Use approximately $40–$90 as a directional planning range. Actual prices can fall outside it because Google varies lead prices by market, job, lead type, and bidding mode.

Does Google publish a towing LSA average?

No national towing-specific average is published by Google. Your own charged-lead and completed-job data is the most useful benchmark.

Are LSAs cheaper than Google Search Ads?

Not automatically. Compare both channels using qualified calls, booked jobs, completed jobs, and revenue rather than their different surface-level billing metrics.

How do I calculate cost per booked job?

Divide LSA spend by the number of booked towing jobs. Then divide spend by completed jobs to account for cancellations and jobs that were not fulfilled.
